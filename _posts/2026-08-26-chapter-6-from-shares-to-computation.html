    Chapter 6: From Shares to Computation

    TL;DR. Add shares locally; multiply needs one interaction (Beaver triple or degree-reduction) — enough to compute anything.
    🎣 The hook Three hospitals want the average recovery time for a treatment. No hospital will reveal its patients' times, and no central server should see them either. Can they learn the average — and *only* the average — with the same hill-and-stakes math that hid the vault key?

    Prerequisites

    • Chapters 1–5: Shamir and additive sharing, Lagrange, threshold signatures' "combine partials" pattern
    • Intuition that addition is local on shares, multiplication is not — no algebra beyond mod p

    By the end you will be able to…

    • Add two shared secrets locally and argue why the result stays hidden
    • Show why multiplying shared secrets raises the polynomial degree and how re-sharing fixes it
    • Use a Beaver triple to turn a multiplication into local work plus one opening
    • Identify which real PET problems are "addition-only" (aggregation) vs need full MPC

    The foundational truth

    Secret sharing is not just storage — it is a computing substrate.

    Shares compose under linear operations for free: add two shared values locally and you hold a share of the sum, with no communication. Multiplication breaks that — the product polynomial has double the degree, needing twice as many points to pin, and you must interact to re-share it.

    That split — free addition, costly multiplication — is the whole price list of multi-party computation (MPC). Everything fancier follows.

    A familiar bridge — secret ballot tally

    Keep the Hill Model, but switch the secret from a vault key to a ballot count. Call it the Ballot Box Model.

    • Input secret → Alice's salary 30, Bob's 50, Carol's 40 — each is a hill with its own flagpole.
    • Share → each person's hill is sliced into stakes and dealt to three tally servers.
    • Compute → servers locally add their stakes (each server sums what it holds), then jointly open only the summed hill's flagpole — the average — never the individual flagpoles.

    Addition is each server stacking papers locally. Multiplication is servers needing to trade notes to correct the hill's bow — that trade is the cost.

    ⚠️ Before we build further — most people leave this bridge thinking…

    "MPC means encrypting inputs and sending them to a trusted server who computes the answer"

    This is wrong because it restores the single point of failure — whoever holds the decryption key sees everything, violating input privacy.

    What's actually true: Inputs stay shared across servers at all times — the computation runs *on* the shares, and only the final result is opened, so no server ever sees a raw input.

    Building the model, one piece at a time

    Free addition — stacking shares locally

    State

    Homomorphism: Shamir and additive shares are both linear. If secret x is shared as f_x(i) and y as f_y(i) (same threshold, same x's), then f_x(i)+f_y(i) mod p is a valid share of x+y mod p. No server talks to another — each adds its two stake heights locally.

    The same holds for additive sharing: (shares of x) + (shares of y) locally sum to shares of x+y. And multiplying a shared secret by a public constant is also local: c·f(i) is a share of c·x.

    Illustrate

    Hospitals A,B,C have salaries 30,50,40. Choose prime p=101, threshold t=2, lines:

    A: fA(x)=30+11x → shares (1,41),(2,52),(3,63)
B: fB(x)=50+ 7x → shares (1,57),(2,64),(3,71)
C: fC(x)=40+ 3x → shares (1,43),(2,46),(3,49)

Server 1 holds 41,57,43 → local sum 41+57+43=141 mod101=40
Server 2 holds 52,64,46 → sum 162 mod101=61
Server 3 holds 63,71,49 → sum 183 mod101=82

Combined sum secret should be 120 mod101=19? Wait 30+50+40=120 mod101=19
Check servers' sums are shares of 19:
Line for sum: f(x)=19+21x (11+7+3=21) → f1=40 ✓ f2=61 ✓ f3=82 ✓

Open: any 2 servers rebuild 19 → 120 → average 40. No server saw 30,50,40.

    Three local additions per server, zero messages. That is why "addition-only" PETs (federated sum, private telemetry) are cheap — they use this path only.

    # Pseudocode: local addition
share_sum[i] = (share_A[i] + share_B[i]) % p
# No communication — same for c*share: (c * share_A[i]) % p
    Can servers locally subtract as well — say, to compute variance Σ(x−mean)² — without revealing x or mean?
    Subtraction is just addition with a negative constant, so yes — each server computes share_of_x minus share_of_mean locally, still no communication. The square needs multiplication, which costs — next scaffold.
    Adding shared numbers is like each tally clerk adding their own pile of ballots without talking — the piles still encode the true total.
    🛑 Pause & reflect

    If addition is free, why can't we compute the sum of squares Σx² the same way by squaring each share locally?

    Expert answerSquaring a share f(i)² gives a point on h(x)=f(x)², degree 2(t−1). Where f needed t points, h needs 2t−1 — the quorum size grew. Without re-sharing to degree t−1, you lose the threshold property. Need interaction to reduce degree.

    Costly multiplication — degree growth and Beaver's shortcut

    State

    The problem: Multiply two shared secrets x=f(i), y=g(i). Local product h(i)=f(i)·g(i) lies on polynomial h(x)=f(x)·g(x) of degree 2(t−1) — needs 2t−1 shares to rebuild, not t. Left as-is, repeated multiplications blow up the threshold until every party is needed.

    Fix 1 — re-sharing / degree reduction: servers reshare their local products via resharing, then combine to a degree-(t−1) sharing of the product — one round of interaction (BGW protocol).

    Fix 2 — Beaver triples: Pre-share random triple (a,b,c) with c=a·b. To multiply shared x,y, open d=x−a and e=y−b (public masks), then each holder computes locally c + d·b + e·a + d·e — a share of x·y. One opening (two public values) replaces resharing.

    Illustrate

    Smallest demo: t=2 lines degree 1 over p=101, secrets x=3 (f=3+2x), y=5 (g=5+4x):

    f(1)=5, f(2)=7, f(3)=9
g(1)=9, g(2)=13, g(3)=17
Local products h(i)=f(i)·g(i): 45,91,153 mod101=45,91,52
h(x)=f·g degree 2: 15+22x+8x² mod101 → h(1)=45 ✓ h(2)=91 ✓

Needs 3 points to open 15 (which is 3·5). Threshold grew from 2 to 3.

    Beaver trick: pre-shared triple (a=10,b=20,c=200 mod101=98 actually 10·20=200 mod101=98). Parties hold shares of a,b,c. To multiply x,y:

    # Each party i computes d_i = share_x[i] − share_a[i], e_i = share_y[i] − share_b[i]
# Open d = reconstruct(d_i), e = reconstruct(e_i)  // public, but masked
# Each party computes share_xy[i] = share_c[i] + d·share_b[i] + e·share_a[i] + (i==0?d·e:0)
# Result is degree-(t−1) sharing of x·y — one opening, no degree blow-up
d = x−a = 3−10=−7 mod101=94,  e=5−20=−15 mod101=86  → public
Share: 98 +94·share_b +86·share_a + 94·86 (to one party)  → reconstructs to 15 ✓

    The masks d,e leak nothing about x,y because a,b are uniform random — one-time pads in the field.

    Could we skip Beaver and just locally multiply, then refresh as in Chapter 4's zero-hill trick?
    No — the hill from squaring is higher-degree, not just a different slope at same degree. Adding a zero-hill (constant 0) does not lower degree; it just re-randomizes the same shape family. Degree reduction needs resharing or Beaver — you must actively move from degree 2(t−1) back to t−1.
    Multiplying shared numbers bends the hill higher — you must jointly iron it back flat before it outgrows the quorum.
    🔴 Deep dive BGW vs SPDZ — honest majority vs dishonest majority

    BGW (Ben-Or–Goldwasser–Wigderson, 1988) uses Shamir plus degree reduction and assumes honest majority (t ≤ n/2 for VSS-reinforced multiplication, t ≤ n/3 for perfect security without broadcast). SPDZ-style protocols keep additive sharing but pre-produce Beaver triples using somewhat homomorphic encryption under no honest majority (even n−1 cheaters tolerated) — triples are authenticated with MACs so a cheater who lies is caught. Trade: BGW is information-theoretic with t

    In the wild

    Two deployment shapes cover most real uses.

    Addition-only — secure aggregation. Google's cross-device federated learning aggregates model updates from phones with additive masking (variant: SecAgg, 2017) — each phone masks its vector with pairwise random pads shared with other phones, the server sees only the sum. No multiplication is needed in the cryptographic layer; the sum is decoded and the model updates. Same for telemetry: Mozilla's Prio splits each measurement into additive shares to two servers, who sum shares locally and open only the aggregate histogram.

    Full MPC — private set intersection, joint credit scoring. When the function needs multiplication (comparisons, divisions), parties run Beaver-based MPC. Example: three hospitals from the hook compute average *and* variance privately — sum uses local addition, variance needs squares (multiplication) → one Beaver opening per squaring. Libraries like MP-SPDZ and SCALE-MAMBA automate Beaver generation in an offline phase, so online cost is one opening per multiply.

    Active recall — Question
    Servers hold Shamir shares of x and y (t=2). They locally add shares to get a sharing of x+y, but locally multiply to get h(i)=f(i)g(i). Why is the second not a share of x·y at the same threshold?
    Answer
    h(x)=f(x)g(x) is degree 2, needs 3 points — threshold grew from 2 to 3. Must re-share/Beaver to reduce back to degree 1.

    Where it breaks

    Degree is one limit. Semi-honest vs malicious behavior is the other.

    ⚠️ Common misconception"MPC guarantees correct output even if parties cheat." Basic BGW/SPDZ with additive shares as described is only semi-honest — a cheater who lies about a share can corrupt the result without being caught. Malicious security adds MACs on shares (SPDZ tags) or VSS commitments per multiplication so deviation is detected and the cheater identified.
    🚫 Edge caseTwo silent breaks: (1) Not enough honest parties for BGW — with Shamir and t>n/2, degree reduction cannot be made information-theoretic; you must switch to SPDZ-style computational security. (2) Opening d,e in Beaver leaks nothing about x,y only if a,b were fresh and random — reusing a triple across two multiplies leaks the relation between inputs.
    🔮 PredictBefore the final chapter: hospitals can MPC the average, but the average itself may leak about one hospital's data (e.g., if you know two inputs). What extra layer would hide *function output* leakage, not just input leakage?

    The whole picture

    The hospitals never revealed their patients' times. Each hospital's number was split into hills dealt to tally servers; servers locally stacked their stakes for the sum and opened only the average — three local additions, zero messages about the inputs.

    When the function needs multiplication — variance, ranking, pricing — local products bend the hill too high. Re-sharing or a Beaver triple (random a·b=c as a mask) brings it back flat with one opening. That single price — one round per multiply — is the bridge from hiding a vault key to computing any function on hidden inputs.

    The ballot box was never opened — only the tally was.

    If you remember only one thing: Shared addition is local; shared multiplication needs one interaction — that one cost powers general MPC.
    🚀 Launch pad · one step beyond

    Private average with one cheater

    Three hospitals run the additive sum demo from this chapter with servers S1,S2,S3 (Shamir t=2). So far all were semi-honest. Now S3 is malicious and, during the sum protocol, adds 10 to its local share sum before opening — shifting the reconstructed total by 10·Li(0). (a) Show the math of the shift using Lagrange coefficients for the opening set {S1,S2} vs {S1,S3}. (b) Propose a one-line fix using Chapter 4's Feldman commitments applied to the *sum sharing* that would catch S3 before opening, and name its cost.

    Stuck? Reveal a hint at a time.
    Hint 1 — reframeOpening with {S1,S2} uses weights (2/(2−1)=2 for S1? compute properly) — the shift from S3 only appears when S3 is in the chosen quorum. But an honest combiner could pick any t — the cheater's power is quorum-dependent without verification.
    Hint 2 — key conceptAdd a post-addition VSS: each server commits to its sum share's polynomial (or uses distributed commitments summed homomorphically). Before opening, verifiers check g^{share_sum_i} vs summed commitments — Feldman per-server proof.
    Hint 3 — setupCost: one exponentiation per verifier plus commitments that grew with degree — exactly Chapter 4's trade: computational hardness for cheater detection. In SPDZ style, this is the MAC check.

    Glossary

    Multi-party computation (MPC)
    Joint evaluation of a function where inputs stay shared and only the output is opened. In the Ballot Model: tally servers compute the total without opening ballots.
    Homomorphism (linear)
    Adding shares locally yields a share of the sum — no interaction. Clerks adding their own piles.
    Degree growth
    Local multiplication makes degree 2(t−1); quorum grows to 2t−1 until re-shared. Hill that outgrows the number of stakes.
    Beaver triple (a,b,c=a·b)
    Pre-shared random multiplication to turn one multiply into one opening of masked values. Random decoy hill whose zero-knowledge sanding brings the real hill flat.
    BGW / SPDZ
    Two MPC families: BGW = honest-majority, information-theoretic; SPDZ = dishonest-majority, computational with MACs. Different dishonesty tolerances.
