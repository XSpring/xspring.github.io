    Chapter 7: The PET Landscape

    TL;DR. Secret sharing hides inputs with shares; DP adds noise to hide outputs; PSI, ZK, HE trade accuracy, interaction, and trust — composition is the real PET.
    🎣 The hook Alice's banks can now share, verify, threshold-sign, and even compute averages without revealing inputs (Chapters 1–6). Then a regulator asks: "Your average recovery time is 41 days — does that leak that Hospital A had very long stays?" Input privacy held, but output privacy did not. Where do shares end and other privacy tools begin?

    Prerequisites

    • Chapters 1–6: Shamir, fields, VSS, threshold crypto, MPC addition vs multiplication price list
    • Comfort with trade-off thinking — no tool gives everything, composition is the norm

    By the end you will be able to…

    • Place secret sharing / MPC on a PET map alongside differential privacy, PSI, HE, ZK
    • Explain why MPC hides inputs but not outputs — and why DP complements it
    • Choose among PETs for four canonical problems (private aggregation, threshold custody, contact discovery, auditable claim)
    • Combine PETs in a layered architecture and name what each layer protects

    The foundational truth

    Every PET hides something, but not the same thing.

    Secret sharing and MPC hide inputs from each other and from the servers — even with unbounded compute (Ch. 3), with thresholds. They do not hide what the output itself reveals about those inputs. If the average is 99 and you know two of three inputs were 0, you infer the third.

    That gap is why PETs come as a family, not a single winner. The foundational truth of this chapter: privacy has two endpoints — hide the inputs (MPC / HE / PSI) and hide the outputs (differential privacy) — and most real systems need both.

    A familiar bridge — envelopes vs. frosted glass vs. blurred photos

    Use three physical hides for the rest of the chapter.

    • Envelopes (secret sharing / MPC) → slip numbers into envelopes dealt to servers; servers tally without opening envelopes, then open only the total.
    • Frosted glass (homomorphic encryption) → one person works behind glass — they can add and multiply shapes they see as shadows without ever opening them.
    • Blurred photos (differential privacy) → publish a photo of the tally, but add a little blur so no single person changes the picture much — hides individuals even from the recipient of the output.

    Two more for completeness: PSI is "do our lists overlap without revealing non-overlaps," and ZK is "prove a claim without showing the evidence."

    ⚠️ Before we build further — most people leave this bridge thinking…

    "`More PETs stacked = more private' — just layer DP, MPC, HE and you get total privacy"

    This is wrong because PETs pull against each other on accuracy, speed, and trust — wrong composition can waste budget or add no real guarantee.

    What's actually true: PETs compose usefully only when each protects a different endpoint (inputs vs outputs vs integrity) — stacking two input-hiding tools without an output-hiding one leaves output leakage untouched.

    Building the model, one piece at a time

    A PET map — what each hides and at what price

    State

    Five major PETs, by the endpoint they protect:

    • MPC (this series: Shamir/Additive + Beaver) hides inputs from other inputs and from servers; exact output. Price: interaction (1 round per multiply) and honest-majority or authenticated triples. Ideal for joint analytics without a trusted server.
    • Homomorphic encryption (HE: BFV, CKKS, TFHE) hides inputs from a single untrusted worker; exact or approximate output; non-interactive (client encrypts, server computes). Price: ciphertext blow-up and slow non-linear ops.
    • Private Set Intersection (PSI) hides inputs except the intersection; special-purpose but very fast (OT-based). Price: only set problems, not general functions.
    • Zero-knowledge proofs (ZK: Groth16, PLONK) hides witness behind a claim — proves "I know a valid input" without revealing it. Price: prover cost, not input hiding from collaborators.
    • Differential privacy (DP) hides any single input's influence on the *output* — the only PET that limits what the recipient of the result can infer. Price: noise trades accuracy for privacy, parameterized by ε (epsilon) (smaller ε = more noise, more hiding).
    Illustrate

    Salaries 30, 50, 40 from Chapter 6:

    • MPC average = (30+50+40)/3 = 40 exactly; no server saw a salary, but the recipient learns 40 — if they know two salaries they learn the third.
    • MPC+DP average = 40 + Lap(Δf/ε) noise, say 40+2 = 42. Now even knowing two salaries, the third is deniable within ε — output privacy. Accuracy loss is the price.
    • HE version: Carol encrypts 40 to server, server homomorphically adds encrypted 30,50 from others, returns encrypted 120 — Carol decrypts. Non-interactive, but server work is heavier.
    • PSI version does not apply — not a set problem.
    # DP layer after MPC (central DP)
true_avg = reconstruct(mpc_shares) / n          # 40
sensitivity = (max_salary - min_salary)/n       # how much one person moves avg
noisy_avg = true_avg + laplace(0, sensitivity/epsilon)
publish(noisy_avg)   # ε=1 → scale = sensitivity, moderate noise
    A team proposes: "We will run MPC on salaries, then add DP noise, but the servers will see the DP noise value — is that a problem?"
    It can be, if noise is added *after* opening to one server. Better to add noise *inside* MPC: parties secret-share Laplace/ Gaussian noise and add it to the shared sum before opening — so no server sees which part was noise vs data. DP inside MPC composes cleanly; DP outside needs a trusted noise-adder.
    Envelopes hide who wrote what; blur hides whose handwriting could have changed the total — you need both to protect a person from the tally's readers.
    🛑 Pause & reflect

    A hospital says "Our MPC sum leaks nothing, so we don't need DP." Name a case where the exact sum alone leaks a participant's secret, even though MPC hid every input from the servers.

    Expert answerIf Alice and Bob collude after learning sum=120 and know Carol's input is in 0…100, they learn Carol has 40 exactly (120−30−50) — MPC hid it from servers, not from output recipients. With repeated queries, a single person's data can be reconstructed. DP would have made each output deniable.

    Choosing among PETs — four canonical puzzles

    State

    Decision pattern (what you hide, from whom, with what cost):

    • Puzzle 1 — Private aggregation (mean salary, telemetry sum) → Additive secret sharing / SecAgg + DP. Why: sum is addition-only, cheap (Ch. 6 first scaffold); DP hides individuals from aggregate readers.
    • Puzzle 2 — Threshold custody (sign a transaction if 3-of-5 agree) → Threshold signatures (Ch. 5), no DP needed. Why: secret is a key, not a dataset — MPC threshold protects the key; output (signature) is meant to be public.
    • Puzzle 3 — Contact discovery ("which of my contacts are on this app?") → PSI (or PIR). Why: set intersection without revealing non-intersection; generic MPC would be overkill.
    • Puzzle 4 — Auditable claim ("all accounts balance without revealing balances") → ZK proof. Why: one prover to many verifiers, hides witness but proves predicate.
    Illustrate

    Federated learning round in practice layers two: phones Shamir-share model deltas to two non-colluding servers (SecAgg additive shares — no Shamir degree needed), servers MPC-sum the shares, then a DP mechanism adds Gaussian noise inside MPC before publishing the new global model. The published model leaks bounded information per user (ε-DP) and no server saw a raw delta.

    Phone → additive shares of delta to ServerA, ServerB
Servers (no MPC mult needed): sum shares locally → shared sum
Inside MPC: shared_sum + Lap(Δf/ε)  → open noisy_sum → update model
Phones never revealed delta; recipients cannot infer one delta from noisy_sum.
    Why not replace secret sharing with HE for federated aggregation — then only one server is needed?
    HE gives single-server, non-interactive aggregation — operationally simpler. Trade: HE ciphertexts are 100–1000× larger and non-linear ops (comparisons, thresholds) are far slower. For sum-only workloads, additive MPC on two non-colluding servers is orders of magnitude faster, which is why SecAgg chose it. HE wins when the operator truly cannot be split into non-colluding halves.
    Pick the envelope for shared work, the blur for published results, and the special cutter only for list problems.
    🔴 Deep dive ε and sensitivity — the DP dial in one minute

    Sensitivity Δf is how much one person's data can change the function (for mean, Δf = range/n). Laplace noise scale = Δf/ε. ε is the budget: ε→0 → huge noise, strong deniability; ε→∞ → no noise, no DP. Repeated queries compose: k queries with ε each spend kε total. That is why central DP inside MPC still tracks a privacy budget across training rounds.

    # mean 0…100, n=100, Δf=1.0
# ε=1.0 → Lap(1.0) σ≈1.4, mild blur on mean 40
# ε=0.1 → Lap(10) σ≈14, strong blur — hides anyone but mean is noisy

    In the wild

    Layered PETs are not academic — they ship in phones and browsers.

    MPC + DP in the wild: Google SecAgg / federated learning runs additive masking across phones with two servers summing shares (Ch. 6 addition path), then DP noise. Mozilla Prio (now Divvi Up) splits telemetry via additive shares to two aggregators — no aggregator sees a raw measurement. Apple's private federated analytics uses a similar two-server split plus local DP.

    Threshold in the wild: The vault and wallet cases from Chapters 2/5 — HashiCorp Vault (Shamir 5-of-3 default) Vault Seal, Ethereum distributed validators (DKG + threshold BLS), custody MPC wallets — all choose threshold not for DP but for single-point-of-failure removal.

    HE vs MPC choice: When contact discovery must hide non-members under a single-server model, OT-based PSI beats generic MPC; when analytics must stay accurate but hide inputs, MPC beats DP alone; when the output must be publishable forever, DP is the only output-hiding PET.

    Active recall — Question
    Your MPC average hid salaries from servers, but a regulator says "the published average 41 still leaks individuals." Which PET did you miss?
    Answer
    Differential privacy on the output — you hid inputs (MPC), not output influence. Add Laplace/Gaussian noise to the opened result before publishing.

    Where it breaks

    Composition has sharper edges than any single PET.

    ⚠️ Common misconception"If we used Shamir with t=n, we don't need DP — n−1 colluders still learn nothing." That protects inputs from colluders within the protocol, not recipients of the output after opening. Once the average is published, all thresholds are gone — DP is about the published artifact, not the sharing phase. Confusing the two is the most common PET misuse.
    🚫 Edge caseTwo composition traps: (1) DP budget exhaustion — repeated noisy averages with fresh MPC but same ε each time accumulate privacy loss (kε) until deniability evaporates; must budget across time. (2) Single-server HE that later runs DP after decryption reintroduces a trusted decryptor — if that server is compromised before noise, inputs leak. Add noise *inside* HE/MPC before any opening.

    The whole picture

    The series hook in Chapter 1 asked: how do you hand out slips that look like gibberish alone yet rebuild 53 together? Additive sharing answered it for "all must meet," Shamir generalized it to "any t," the clock made the counting uniform, receipts made it trustworthy, threshold crypto removed the rebuild window, MPC let hospitals average without revealing, and this chapter blurred the published average so recipients cannot infer a single stay.

    Secret sharing was the root primitive — not because it solves privacy alone, but because it is the composable input-hiding layer that every other PET assumes when they say "parties." The vault combination, the choir's chord, the ballot tally, and the blurred photo are the same idea at different endpoints: spread trust so no one place is enough, then hide what even the answer would reveal.

    From envelope to blur — the series ends where privacy does: at composition.

    If you remember only one thing: Use shares to hide inputs, noise to hide outputs — compose them, never alone.

    Glossary

    Privacy-Enhancing Technology (PET)
    Umbrella for tools that hide data at some endpoint. Family: MPC, HE, PSI, ZK, DP — envelopes, glass, blur, intersection, and proof.
    MPC / Secret-shared computation
    Hides inputs, exact output, interactive (1 round per multiply). Envelopes tallied without opening.
    Homomorphic encryption (HE)
    Hides inputs from a single worker, non-interactive, heavier. Frosted glass — one worker adds shadows.
    Private Set Intersection (PSI)
    Hides inputs except overlap. Find common contacts without revealing others.
    Zero-knowledge proof (ZK)
    Hides witness, proves predicate. Prove the tally was honest without opening ballots.
    Differential privacy (DP), ε
    Adds noise to hide any one input's effect on the output; ε tunes trade-off. Blur on the published photo.
    Composition
    Layering PETs — e.g., MPC for inputs + DP for outputs + ZK for integrity. Envelope then blur then receipt.
